\section{Additional Design Considerations}

\subsection{Condition-wise Sinkhorn drifting}
The central transport point is that channel simulation is conditional.
Recent W-Flow work replaces heuristic drifting fields with Sinkhorn-divergence Wasserstein-gradient-flow velocities, but an unrestricted joint transport on \((x,y)\) is not the right object when the transmitted-symbol marginal is fixed.
For channels, only \(p(y\mid x)\) or \(p(e\mid x)\) is transported.
The corresponding conditional Sinkhorn objective is
\[
\mathcal S_\varepsilon^{\mathrm{cond}}(q,p)=
\int S_\varepsilon(q_x,p_x)\,\mu(dx),
\]
with condition-wise velocity
\[
v_x(y)=T^\varepsilon_{q_x,p_x}(y)-T^\varepsilon_{q_x,q_x}(y).
\]
This explains why naive joint Sinkhorn on \((x,y)\) can differ from the proposed condition-wise field even when Sinkhorn improves the unconditional W-Flow setting.

\subsection{End-to-end learned communication}
The learned channel models are also evaluated as differentiable channel implants inside end-to-end communication pipelines.
In that setting, the relevant question is not only whether the generated sample cloud has low SWD, but whether training through the surrogate produces an encoder and decoder that perform well on the analytic or measured target channel.
Direct drifting, residual drifting, joint Sinkhorn, and condition-wise Sinkhorn therefore need SER/BER or BLER checks alongside generator-level metrics.
All one-shot channel implants are differentiable, but they induce different gradients with respect to the transmitted symbol, which can affect transmitter and receiver optimization differently across channel regimes.

\subsection{Parameterization and metric-space analysis}
Direct and residual drifting behave differently across channel classes and evaluation spaces.
The manuscript therefore treats target parameterization and evaluation space as first-class variables: direct-output versus residual targets, direct-output versus residual SWD, anchor-conditioned diagnostics, and downstream coding metrics.
This makes clear when residual parameterization is a useful near-identity inductive bias and when direct-output modeling is preferable.
It also makes explicit that global SWD is not always aligned with coding performance.

\subsection{Extended channel studies}
The core generator-level reference comparison covers AWGN, Rayleigh, and SSPA.
The drift-field and downstream studies additionally include TDL as a wireless channel with memory.
This provides a first test of whether the relative behavior of kernel drifting, condition-wise Sinkhorn drifting, diffusion, and WGAN remains stable once the channel law departs from a memoryless componentwise perturbation.

\subsection{Latency-quality tradeoffs}
The reference comparison covers DDPM, DDIM, and WGAN channel generators.
The drifting family is positioned within the broader space of latency-conscious generative modeling, including reduced-step DDIM, consistency-style generators, distillation-based one-step diffusion, and Sinkhorn-driven one-shot drift fields.
The useful comparison is therefore not only final fidelity, but also inference latency, training cost, and deployment cost.

\subsection{Limitations and design guidance}
Relevant limitations include the dependence of reported quality on metric space, the suitability of residual parameterization for near-identity versus strongly nonlinear channels, and the role of minibatch field estimation.
Exact condition-wise transport also requires repeated samples at the same condition, which is available for simulators but not for arbitrary measured channels.
For measured data, the practical approximation is local conditional Sinkhorn, where nearby conditions are grouped or kernel-weighted before solving the output-space Sinkhorn problem.
